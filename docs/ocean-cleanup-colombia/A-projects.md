# The Ocean Cleanup: Colombia and Latin America

Brief for Gideon (CleantechHUB), 16 October 2026. Research only. As of the latest public releases, Colombia has memoranda and surveys. No source shows an Interceptor, Barrier, or Trashfence already operating there.

## Colombia

**Santa Marta — MoU, 19 August 2026.** The city and The Ocean Cleanup agreed to deploy tailor-made Interceptors on the Río Manzanares and Río Gaira, plus downstream cleanups, before plastic reaches Caribbean reefs. CORPAMAG (the Magdalena regional environmental authority) is named for coordination, permits, and a possible link to its Green Business Program. The organization calls this its 13th country and part of 30 Cities. Status: agreement only. [1]

**Barranquilla — MoU, 21 September 2026.** Signed with the Mayor’s Office (Alejandro Char; Ricardo Restrepo, Regional Director for the Americas). Floating Interceptors are under consideration for the lower Magdalena, in its last stretch before the sea, and Arroyo León, to protect Ciénaga de Mallorquín, mangroves, and beaches. Char described the city’s existing trash trap on Arroyo León as municipal work, not an Ocean Cleanup unit. A March 2026 funding note had already listed Barranquilla for a 2026 deployment; the September release still treats sites as candidates. [2][3]

**Cartagena — survey started 24 June 2026.** The Mayor’s Office, its international-cooperation office, and EPA (Mauricio Rodríguez Gómez) opened a pilot with Restrepo under an MoU. Teams are measuring ten canals (Matute–Pedro Romero/Palmeras, Ricaurte–San Antonio, Calicanto Nuevo/Carioca, El Tigre, Playa Blanca, Pinzón, Santa Clara, Barcelona, Chiamaría, San Pablo) before choosing a technology. The city ties this to 30 Cities. No matching release was found on theoceancleanup.com. The MoU signature date is not stated. [4]

The path in started earlier: on 15 February 2025 the Dutch embassy and Mayor Dumek Turbay announced a May 2025 exploratory visit at which The Ocean Cleanup and Bubble Barrier would present canal technologies (Interceptor Barrier, Tender, Barricade, Guard). That note is a presentation plan, not an installation. Whether the May visit happened was not confirmed. [5]

**Not found.** The Colombia releases above do not name the Atrato, a port authority, a private company, or the Atlántico CAR (CRA) or Cartagena’s CARDIQUE. No other public source found in this pass does either.

## Wider region

**Guatemala.** Motagua-basin work dates to 2019. Trashfence Interceptor 006 was trialed on the Río Las Vacas in May 2022, damaged by a flood, then operating as a barricade by May 2023; more than 10 million kg stopped since. Interceptor 021 (Barricade XL, El Quetzalito, Motagua mouth) went in during 2024 with environment ministry MARN, which sorts the catch. A second barrier in 2025 let 021 span the full river. The 2025 report says over 20 million kg a year used to reach the Gulf of Honduras, and mapping began for a 130 km coastal sweep of the Guatemala–Honduras shore. [6][7][8]

**Honduras (same gulf).** Interceptor 023 was deployed in 2025 on the Río Chamelecón, which carries leakage from San Pedro Sula. Exact in-water date not published. [8]

**Jamaica — the city model.** Visits from 2019. With Clean Harbours Jamaica and the GraceKennedy Foundation (Benioff Foundation funding), barriers went in during 2022 at Kingston Pen, Barnes, Rae Town, and Tivoli (008–011). 012–014 followed in 2023. By January 2024 seven units had finished phase one, aimed at about 1,000 tons a year; 015 (Sandy Gully, Hunt’s Bay) in 2024 and 016 (Balmagie Gully) in 2025. Montego Bay was named in June 2025 for a later citywide extension. [9][10][11][8]

**Dominican Republic.** Interceptor Original 004 was deployed on the Ozama at Santo Domingo in 2020 under Rescate Ozama (Dominican government, Dutch embassy, UNDP, ECORED; the Navy operated it) and stopped over 300 tonnes. In 2025 it was withdrawn and is being turned into an education classroom. On 13 August 2026 a replacement was announced: a split 400 m barrier, their largest, with a navigation gap and capacity for water hyacinth, targeted for early 2027, as the first 30 Cities step in the country. A February 2025 government note saying operations began in late 2021 conflicts with the 2020 date used by The Ocean Cleanup. [12][13][14][21]

**Panama.** On 10 March 2025 Boyan Slat and Marea Verde launched Siete Cuencas (seven urban rivers into Panama Bay), with the mayor and environment minister present; the YP Foundation backed the first unit. The Ocean Cleanup’s 2025 report calls Interceptor 022, in Panama City, the first of those deployments. Marea Verde dates a Río Abajo barrier to June 2025, reports over 85,000 kg there, and calls it the project’s second barrier — that “second” does not line up with 022 being described as the first. By mid-2026 they also cited Matías Hernández (May 2026, over 17,000 kg by July) and Juan Díaz, with four deployments still scheduled. The 2025 report estimates over 800,000 kg a year once all seven run. Rivers named by Marea Verde: Juan Díaz, Matías Hernández, Río Abajo, Matasnillo, Curundú, Tocumen–Tapia, Río Cabra. [15][16][17][8][18]

**Ecuador, Peru, Mexico.** Not named in the deployment FAQ, the 2025 annual report, the June 2025 city list, or the March 2026 deployment list. Ichthion (San Pedro, Ecuador) and WILDCOAST (Tijuana) appear under the Clean Currents Coalition, not The Ocean Cleanup. That is an absence from these sources, not proof that quiet scoping does not exist. The FAQ itself is stale: it omits Panama. [19][8][11][3][20]

## 30 Cities, and where Latin America sits

Announced 12 June 2025 at the UN Ocean Conference in Nice. The shift is from one river to every major emitting waterway in a city, because Kingston showed one partner set can cover a whole city. Modeling says 30 coastal cities in Asia and the Americas could stop up to one third of river plastic by 2030, a step toward removing 90 percent of floating ocean plastic by 2040. Each city starts with drones, AI imagery, and GPS-tagged dummy plastics, then Interceptors plus coastal sweeps. Program cost is given as USD 350 million. At launch the only Latin American names were Panama City and, as an expansion of existing work, Montego Bay. Kingston, Guatemala, and the Gulf of Honduras were described as the current foundation, not as newly announced cities. [11][3][8]

On 9 March 2026 The Audacious Project (TED) committed USD 121 million of that 350 million. Cities named for 2026 deployments include Barranquilla, Santo Domingo, and Panama City. Santa Marta and Cartagena came later and are not on that March list. Latin America is the working half of the “Americas” side: a city model (Kingston), a gulf system (Guatemala and Honduras), a live 30 Cities city (Panama), a restart (Santo Domingo), and a 2026 opening in South America (Colombia). [3][1][4]

## 2025–2026 in one pass

River catch in 2025 was 27.3 million kg, 131 percent above 2024. New units: 016 (Jamaica), 022 (Panama City), 023 (Honduras), and the second barrier on Guatemala’s 021. Cumulative trash, oceans included, was 47.8 million kg at the start of 2026 and “more than 62 million kg” by the September 2026 Barranquilla release. [8][2]

**Still unverified:** any operating unit on the Magdalena, Atrato, or in Cartagena, Barranquilla, or Santa Marta; any Colombian port, company, CRA, or CARDIQUE partner; the Cartagena MoU date; the exact date of Honduras 023; whether the announced May 2025 Cartagena visit happened; whether Juan Díaz is a full Interceptor or a lighter barrier; why Marea Verde calls the June 2025 Río Abajo barrier the second.
