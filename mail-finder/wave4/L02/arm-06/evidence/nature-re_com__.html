According to climate scientists at the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), limiting global warming to 1.5°C requires removing 5 to 10 gigatons of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere every single year by 2050.

Nature-based solutions present a promising approach to addressing climate change. Restoring native forests turns degraded land into a massive carbon sink, actively removing carbon from the atmosphere. 

Wildlife population has decreased by 95% in Latin America since 1970.The destruction of forests, grasslands and wetlands, overexploitation and alien species have all contributed to this sharp decline.

Assisted Natural Regeneration recovers native plant species. Allowing forest regrowth increases wildlife habitat connectivity.

Local community involvement is essential for nature restoration. It ensures that projects are culturally relevant, economically sustainable, and tailored to the specific ecological challenges of the area.

Social impact is embedded to support lasting shared value through our HEART strategy,  focusing on job creation, environmental education, and inclusion.

The recent assessment of Earth’s planetary boundaries identified green water, the soil moisture available for vegetation, as one of the critical Earth system processes that has already exceeded its safe operating space.

Restoring native forest cover is essential for recovering hydrological processes. Protecting vital micro-watersheds and hamlet aqueducts through targeted landscape restoration, improves regional hydrological quality and secures local water sources.
