Welcome!

7

Amazon Investor Coalition: 2024-2025 Biennial Report

Founded in 2020, the Amazon
Investor Coalition is a global,
non-profit learning-andcollaboration platform that
unites investors, philanthropists,
and corporate buyers in
partnership with Amazon
communities, entrepreneurs,
governments, and civil society.
We serve as a catalyst by
improving the capillarity
between Amazonians and circles
of capital around the world.

Our mission is to
shift Amazonian
economies away
from deforestation
and towards forest
protection.
In 2024, we transitioned from
serving primarily as a convener,
to also serving as a grantmaker.

We deployed $1.5M+
in grants in the past
2 years.

Over this period, we have tested
assumptions, honed strategies,
and produced this biennial
report about both our lessons
learned and our impact.
Directly and indirectly as a result
of our work, our partners were
able to mobilize significant new
financing in the past two years.
Though the final grant sizes and
details are still being negotiated
at the time of this writing, the
total amount of new funding
mobilized exceeds $15 million.

Introduction
and
background

9

Amazon Investor Coalition: 2024-2025 Biennial Report

In February, 2024, scientists
reported that reforestation of
5% of the Amazon is needed
to prevent the region from
collapsing into a dry grassland
ecosystem.
Collapse could threaten 10% of
the world’s species, start new
pandemics, destabilize global
water cycles, release over 100gt
of CO2, put global food security
at risk, and more.

Meanwhile, even
though deforestation
in Brazil has dropped
by 50% since 2022
(11% in 2025 and 31%
in 2024), regional
forest fires reached
record levels.
While there is progress on
paper, forest stress is high,
criminal activity is rising, and
local economic drivers still favor
deforestation. And, given the
historic retreat of USAID in early
2025, fresh funding sources are
sorely needed.

Given these realities, since
2024, we have prioritized the
development of catalytic
activities that can help stabilize
the rule of law, build highintegrity regional supply chains,
secure financing and help the
forest-protection economy
to replace the deforestation
economy.

Regenerative. Catalytic. At scale.
With these objectives in mind,
we are working to demonstrate
that by uniting investment,

As a first step, we mapped the

Philanthropy

We also conducted
donor education efforts
at philanthropy-related
conferences like NEXUS, TED, and
the World Economic Forum.

One recent study
estimated that
between $1.7 and
$2.8 billion per year
is needed for Amazon
conservation, in
Brazil alone.
The available funding
continuously comes up short.
Each year, approximately $700
million is donated, with about
$500 million coming from
governments and $200 million
from philanthropy. To fill the gap,
we will need to overcome issues
of indecision, uncertainty about
impact, fear of mistakes, cultural
hesitation, donor fatigue and
more.

Along the way, in 2024, we
discovered that grant money
was left on the sidelines because
the right information was not
reaching the right audiences.
To tackle the issue in 2025, we
created a new peer-to-peer
network for prospective highlevel donors to connect with their
more experienced counterparts
and support the arrival of new
generosity in the region.
To date, over 50 grantmakers
and high-net-worth individuals
have joined the new Amazonia
Philanthropy Network. The
process meanwhile, has

Investment
When the AIC began, we
surveyed investors, connected
them with investable
opportunities and conducted
a landscape analysis about
investing in the region. While high
value opportunities were rare, we
realized that hype about them

and drive local entrepreneurial

We learned that more

and prime the pump
of commercial-grade

both protect the

investment.
These discoveries helped

The most significant AIC-led
offerings to innovative finance

involve blended finance,
revenue based financing,
ecosystem service payments,

Corporate

bioeconomy is replete with
examples of forest communities

Correspondingly, we sought

third key financing pillar, for

Early on we mapped prices,

Finally, in addition to improving
the landscape for buyers, the

investments in supporting
suppliers and regional
bioeconomy development at
large.

Capital Alignment
Multiplier
By uniting the interests of
philanthropy, investment and
corporate procurement into an
integrated strategy, we foster
synergies.
Procurement support increases
sales. Sales improve community
investability. Investability
reduces financial dependency.
And, thus the flywheel spins helping bioeconomies to replace
deforestation economies.

Opportunity
Deforestation in the
Amazon is driven by
economic incentives
that make clearing
land for agriculture,
logging, and mining
more profitable than
preserving the forest.
To reduce the influence of such
financial drivers, the Amazon
bioeconomy needs to scale. One
2021 study calculated that the
region supplies only 0.002% of
a global $175 billion market of
64 forest compatible products,
despite being the largest tropical
forest in the world.
A separate 2021 study, by the
AIC, estimated that replacing
Amazonian cattle and soy
with agroforestry, could swell
local incomes from $20
billion to $206 billion a year,
while restoring tree canopies,
improving water cycles and
sequestering carbon. The
potential for growth is huge.

Amazonian bioeconomies are
poised to scale across several
distinct verticals, including
wild harvesting, ecological
restoration, carbon/ecosystemservice-payments, and more.
For donors concerned about
impact-per-dollar, wild
harvesting of commercial
products from pristine forest
is a top priority because
conservation is more cost
effective than restoration. For
investors, ecological restoration
strategies are a top priority
because they represent the
fastest pathway to profitability.
Other bioeconomic
development strategies, such
as pharmacological exploration
and carbon financing, are longer
term, and may depend more on
regulation.

Wild Harvest
In 2024, we prioritized wild
harvesting with our first set of
grants, deploying over $500,000
to unlock capabilities for key
value chains such as Brazil
nuts, tonka beans, arapaima
(pirarucu), cocoa, rubber, and
more.

We invested across different
models of community-led

To fill gaps in the bioeconomy

made further investments of
over $200,000 in supply chain

Several of the grantees we

Ecuadorean Amazon.
Also significant, the startup
MazoMana commercializes 14

protect 10 million hectares of
forest, if successful.

Traceability and Price
Premiums
One of our top partners, since
2024, is Imaflora, a longstanding
supply chain traceability and
certification agency.
Together with their Origens
Brasil network, we helped bring
market access, traceability and
integrity to 54 conservation units
and indigenous territories of
the Brazilian Amazon, helping to
conserve 62 million hectares
of standing forest.
We also began collaborating
on new pricing models for wild
harvest products, helping them
to earn premiums by recognizing
their contributions to ecosystem
services.
A flagship financial mechanism
to cover these premiums is
expected to be launched by
September, 2026.

Training
Early in 2025, we expanded our
team, monitored progress, and
identified catalytic investment
opportunities where a little bit of
funding could go a long way, and
unlock select market capabilities
and investments for many.
One key issue we identified
was the absence of skilled
wild harvesting knowledge
across many commercially
important species. In response,
we convened a consortium of
forest knowledge providers and
supported the development of
a training program led by the
Tropical Forest Institute.
We committed $90,000 and
helped to mobilize nearly $1
million more from the Jacobs
Futura Foundation and others,
in an effort to train over 10,000
people in a 10-year project.

Ecological Restoration
Later in 2025, we also began
to expand our investments in
the economies of ecological
restoration. Even though
silvopasture, intensified

production and other cattle
ranching sustainability
improvements are growth
opportunities in the region, we
prioritized agroforestry because
of its higher profitability and
contributions to the water cycle
and biodiversity conservation.
We selected the Alliance for the
Restoration of the Amazon as a
strategic partner and operational
platform because of its history,
knowledge, membership and
reach.
Earlier in 2024, we also invested
in other restoration related
efforts, such as the Redario
Initiative, which convenes and
supports 27 community-led
native-species seed collection
networks, uniting over 2,500
collectors.
We also supported the
ClimAccelerator, of Quintessa,
an incubation program for
restoration-related startups
that offer ecological restoration
economy solutions.
The program accelerated
several startups covering
solutions ranging from seedling
production and fire-fighting

Intermediaries
We also identified the need to
support market intermediaries
to help suppliers professionalize
their production processes and
improve their market readiness.
Early on, we supported Tucum

A first export was possible in

Finally, we also began to

Networks
Developing communities-ofpractice has been a core AIC
strategy from the start. After

the AIC began to support third

Today, we participate in many

In 2024, we became a founding

Carbon

Finance

Carbon markets and paymentsfor-ecosystem-services are
vital components of Amazonian
bioeconomies. In late 2024, Brazil
legislated its carbon market for
the first time, creating a 5 year
runway to structure the system.
In 2024, the AIC surveyed carbon
projects throughout the Amazon,
discovered multiple irregularities,
and settled on market integrity
as the top priority.

Financial innovation and access
to capital are also essential
ingredients of bioeconomy
expansion. To contribute, we
helped design and fund two new
debt instruments that each bring
unique innovations to finance in
the region.

Correspondingly, in October,
2025, we released a position
paper on Building Trust, Scaling
Impact: An Integrity Ecosystem
for Carbon in the Amazon, to
share our vision for the sector.
Then in November, 2025, at
COP30, together with the
Voluntary Carbon Market
Integrity Initiative we published
the study Best Practices for
High Integrity Carbon Projects in
Brazilian Amazon. Both studies
serve as international references
for investors, entrepreneurs and
other allies working on carbon
ventures in the region.

Fundo Estimulo Amazonia is a
BRL R$100 million private credit
fund that uses blended financing
and AI to automate analysis of
bank statements to expedite
loans for small and medium
Amazonian enterprises.
Relatedly, the BRL R$50
million Amazon Food&Forest
Fund aims to transform the
bankability of community
projects into investable
opportunities by aggregating
suppliers and offtakers for the
commercialization of wildharvest products from the
Amazon region, through the
offering of working capital and
receivables financing.
Both interventions offer patient
and affordable financing to
Amazonian economies, playing
a crucial role in supporting the

Market Analysis
In 2025, to present a vision for
Amazon bioeconomy market
reform, the AIC published
the study From Showcase to
Territory: Invisible Infrastructure
and the Future of the Amazon
Bioeconomy. The study argued
that forest-based economies
cannot achieve scale with justice
and permanence, without the
“invisible infrastructure” that
precedes production.
Common to mature markets,
such infrastructure includes
strong land security, logistics,
connectivity, technical
assistance, trust networks, local
governance, and fit-for-purpose
financial instruments.
This perspective guides the
bioeconomy development
strategies of the AIC, and
others, reinforcing support for
traceability, certification, market
intermediaries, forest knowledge
training, and other preproductive and pre-competitive
conditions necessary for a
forest-protection economy to

thrive.

Market Readiness
and Conclusion
Our investments, partnerships
and overall work from the past
two years - across verticals of
wild harvesting, supply chain
traceability, forest knowledge
training, ecological restoration,
intermediaries, practitioner
networks, carbon integrity,
innovative finance and market
analysis - have improved
Amazonian market readiness
and positioned the AIC as
a strategic partner, funder,
knowledge holder and catalytic
convener.
From this position of leverage,
we hope to catalyze more
investment in the region, as
we bring our discoveries to
ever wider circles of future
prospective Amazonian
philanthropies, investors, and
buyers.
With the proper support, soon the
market readiness of the forestprotection economy will help it
to outcompete the deforestation
economy, and eventually replace
it, one hectare at a time.

Legal
integrity

Legal insecurity in the Amazon
is one of most significant,
and least appreciated drivers
of deforestation. By many

estimates, over 90% of
deforestation in the region is
illegal.
Paradoxically, rule-of-law
issues also frighten away many
donors, even when they are
needed most. Such issues also
frighten away investors and
entrepreneurs, who seek stable
and predictable markets.
To tackle these issues, the
AIC conducted research
about possible philanthropic
interventions to improve
regional governance and law
enforcement. Since 2024, we
have convened peers, built
networks, and started to fund
research and strategic partner
NGOs.
One study we funded, with
the Igarape Institute, titled
“Under the Radar,” found that
investment in the Amazon is
heavily constrained by territorial
insecurity, criminal activity, and
fragmented governance, which
increase operational risk and

deter capital.
A subsequent study we
supported with the LACLIMA
Institute, titled “Sustainable
Investments in the Amazon,
Volume I,” showed that even
where security risks are lower,
legal uncertainty - especially
unclear land tenure, shifting
regulations, and inconsistent
enforcement - creates
significant barriers to investment.
These issues need to be
addressed to enable large-scale,
sustainable investment in the
Amazon.
For our other grants in the
region, we sought to enable
capabilities where they did
not exist previously. Many
forest communities and small
bioeconomy enterprises in the
Amazon do not have access to
legal expertise, remain part of
the informal economy, and fall
out of legal compliance, or suffer
criminal abuse as a result.
To help address some of these
issues in the Brazilian state of
Para, we funded the Maparajuba
Collective to support grassroots

24

legal education, and promote
environmental justice.
To support the wider region, we
co-founded and funded the
Legal Alliance for the Amazon
to promote dialogue and
cooperation among different
actors in the region to help
strengthen the rule of law, scale
existing initiatives and foster new
collective strategies.
At the international level, we also
aided the planning and
fundraising efforts of the Global
Initiative against Transnational
Organized Crime, GI-TOC.
Though the AIC served as one
of several contributors to a GITOC fundraising appeal to the
Norwegian International Climate
and Forest Initiative, the effort
helped to secure a large multiyear grant agreement.
Though originally negotiated
in 2025, on May 11, 2026, the
Norwegian government
announced a grant to GI-TOC of
kr120,000,000 NOK, equivalent
to $13.04 million USD at the
time.

The program will produce and
share intelligence on forest
crimes.
As a result of our interventions,
select decision-makers now
have greater awareness about
legal challenges in the region,
and many Amazonians now
have access to legal knowledge
and assistance for the first time.

The AIC was founded in 2020,

governments are dynamic, and
require consistent engagement
from investors, donors, and
other civil society actors, to
ensure responsible long term
sustainable development.
