## Project Summary

Climate shocks and EUDR exclusion threaten women-led cacao and coffee cooperatives in Colombia’s PDET territories. When harvest income collapses, producers are pushed to clear forest. PDET-CIDM fuses Sentinel-2 and DIST-ALERT with CleantechHUB’s PDET warehouse to score climate-emergency and deforestation pressure, then triggers a 14-day bundle: community-validated geolocation, adaptation support, and buyer matchmaking. CleantechHUB and PASO Colombia will pilot with live cooperatives in Arauca, Guaviare, Istmina, Florida, and Fonseca. Expected results: 400 people supported (≥50% women or youth), USD 75,000 investments mobilised, EUDR dossiers, and standing-forest verification.

## Problem Statement

Colombia’s 170 PDET municipios remain climate and market frontlines. The unaddressed barrier is not the existence of satellite alerts or of field programmes. It is the missing link between a **named climate emergency** that collapses harvest income and a **timed, community-owned response** that keeps legal agroforestry standing and saleable.

**Evidence.** PASO’s August 2026 field inventory — not the May concept-note list — is the operational map. Global Forest Watch (2002–2025) shows active primary-forest loss in the municipios PASO can actually reach: San José del Guaviare 140 kha; Arauquita 14 kha (29% of remaining primary forest lost); Tame 7.1 kha; Istmina 1.1 kha; Florida 69 ha; Fonseca 54 ha. EUDR applies to cacao and coffee from December 2026 (large operators) and June 2027 (smallholders). Most PDET smallholders still lack plot-level, deforestation-free evidence since December 2020.

**Resilience of what.** Household livelihoods in legal cacao, coffee and Amazonian products; cooperative governance; and standing forest in 5 km buffers around participating plots.

**Resilience to what.** Specific climate emergency events — IDEAM precipitation anomalies, drought, flood, and harvest failure — that cut income and historically precede clearing. Secondary stresses: EUDR exclusion and conflict-related access constraints.

**Resilience for whom.** At least 400 women- and youth-majority producers in PASO-linked organisations, with ≥50% women or youth. Priority organisations with known membership: COOPCACAO (132), COMPROCAR (150), Filimarpaz (43) in Arauca; COOAMACOL (68) and ASOMUJAPAL (30 women) in Guaviare; COOMPAZCOL (180) and ASMUPERIJA (16 women coffee growers) in Fonseca. Las Caprichosas and COOMFASOL are women-led Guaviare organisations whose member counts PASO will confirm before submission. Istmina partners (Bioconversión Chocó, Aserrío Continental) and the Florida indigenous cabildo are Track C follow-up sites, not a claim on 4,000 CORE1 people.

The May application named eight MEC 2026 ethnic initiatives in Pacífico Nariñense, Chocó and Putumayo. PASO does not staff that set for this grant. This draft uses the August tracks.

## Solution Statement

**Elevator pitch.** PDET-CIDM is an anticipatory-action layer that turns public satellite and climate signals into a 14-day, community-validated response for PASO-linked cooperatives.

CleantechHUB already operates the PDET warehouse (108,671 records across 170 municipios; live API at pdet-api.cleantechhub.net) and Climate Innovation Data Matching. This grant builds a cooperative-level scoring layer on that warehouse. Inputs: Copernicus Sentinel-2 (10 m, 5-day revisit), NASA/UMD DIST-ALERT, IDEAM precipitation anomalies, EUDR exposure, and women/youth leadership. When the score crosses a calibrated threshold, the consortium delivers: (a) EUDR-ready geolocation from existing polygons and participatory mapping, not external GPS brigades; (b) climate-adaptation support through PASO’s Escuelas Rurales Alternativas; (c) deforestation-free buyer matchmaking; (d) climate-finance matching.

**Why this beats the status quo.** Global Forest Watch alerts without a field owner. FNC SICA geolocates coffee after the fact. Humanitarian anticipatory action targets weather extremes, not commodity-driven clearing. No other actor in these municipios joins alert + warehouse + 14-day PASO response + EUDR dossier + buyer.

**Advantage in this context.** PASO already holds relationships, some polygons, and security protocols. The method agreed in August is progressive georeferencing: satellite and existing records first; participatory mapping in cooperative seats and municipal centres; local verification only when EUDR requires it, after a formal security assessment, by people the community already knows. No contact with armed groups.

**Current state vs this grant.** Warehouse and PASO field presence exist. The grant pays for the scoring layer, the 12-month four-phase pilot, PASO’s USD 20,000 field package, one GRP Induction Workshop trip to Nairobi, and the evidence GRP will audit.

## Thematic Challenge Alignment

Do not repeat the concept-note paragraph. This draft answers the TECH prompts.

**Primary window — TECH4Nature.** The innovation is AI and remote sensing used to protect and manage nature around live agroforestry organisations. DIST-ALERT and Sentinel-2 detect emerging degradation. PASO and the cooperatives validate polygons without sending outside GPS brigades. Repeat passes over 5 km buffers verify that forest stays standing. EUDR dossiers turn regulation into a reason to keep forest, not a reason to abandon legal crops.

**Secondary window — TECH4Emergencies.** Reviewer feedback (14 July 2026) required a **specific climate emergency**, not structural deforestation alone. The trigger is therefore a climate event that can be named and dated: IDEAM precipitation anomaly, drought, flood, or harvest failure. Pathway: climate shock → income collapse → clearing risk → DIST-ALERT confirms pressure → 14-day bundle. Post-conflict PDET status explains vulnerability; it is not the emergency.

**AI value proposition and specs.** The model scores four classes (climate emergency, deforestation alert, EUDR exposure, women/youth leadership) at organisation and, where data exist, plot level. Use case: PASO and CTH review scored cases monthly; PASO activates local liaisons only for prioritised organisations. Expertise: CTH built and runs the warehouse and matching framework; PASO has 10+ years in the same value chains (Arauca cacao/honey with BID Lab; Redes de Paz in Guaviare; ERA platform).

RAIN is not ticked on the form in this draft. The nature-positive and rural-livelihoods content sits inside TECH4Nature. Add RAIN later only if PASO wants the extra checkbox.

## Scaling Pathway and Additionality

A scaled version is the same scoring layer operating across the 16 PDET subregions, with PASO or peer field partners owning the 14-day bundle. The model is “data already live + local partner already trusted,” not a new national platform.

Demand is time-bound: EUDR dates and climate volatility. PASO’s Arauca cacao work already sold 4,164 kg at above-market prices and raised women-led cacao incomes 149% in the BID Lab project — evidence that these organisations will use a compliance and market tool if it does not send strangers to their plots.

Pathway: open methodology (EN/ES); ART conversations on Climate Innovation Data Matching; buyer files PASO can reuse in Alianzas Comerciales Colaborativas.

**Additionality.** GRP pays for the anticipatory layer and the PASO cash package. It does not replace PASO’s in-kind field presence or CTH’s warehouse. The May LOI’s ~USD 33,000 in-kind remains complementary, not a line in the GRP sheet.

**Indicative 2028–2029 targets.** 2,000 people supported; USD 400,000 mobilised; replication in one additional PDET subregion.

## Sustainability, Profitability and Exit

Non-profit model. Producers keep 15–30% EUDR / specialty premiums. CTH covers residual scoring cost (~USD 800/month) from Climate Innovation Data Matching and complementary grants (P4G, UNDP-AFCIA, GIZ, Climate-KIC track record). PASO continues ERA / Redes de Paz regardless of GRP.

Fundraising: CTH 2025 statements are signed and audited (Soluciones JV; Revisor Fiscal LG Asesores Contables SAS). PASO sits under One Earth Future financial rules. Exit by December 2027: CTH operates the layer; PASO owns community relationships and dossiers; no second GRP grant required.

Value for money: USD 50,000 buys a tested trigger in five departments PASO already reaches, versus rebuilding presence in Nariño/Putumayo.
